# Generation — territories and anti-patterns

## Semantic territories

Explore deeply, but **do not force the final name to literally represent any
of them**. The best names carry meaning the company builds into them later.

precision · mastery · craft · construction · structure · systems · order ·
architecture · foundations · tools · direction · movement · navigation ·
guidance · clarity · light · knowledge · discovery · connection ·
composition · transformation · capability · potential · future

Also explore words that are completely unexpected for the category. Apple
does not mean computer. Stripe does not mean payments. Canon does not mean
camera.

## The four categories

Track these separately so the longlist does not collapse into one style.

### A — Real words
Existing words used in an unexpected context. Highest brandability ceiling,
lowest domain availability. Expect to buy the `.com`.

### B — Rare words
Legitimate but low-frequency words. Better availability, but verify the word
is actually natural to native speakers — obscure is not the same as elegant.

### C — Linguistic roots
Latin, Greek, Portuguese, Spanish, Tupi-Guarani or others — **only when
there is a real reason**. Document the source and gloss for every one. A
root you cannot cite is a neologism, not a root.

### D — Grounded neologisms
Permitted only when **all** of these hold:

- derived from real roots
- phonetically natural in every target language
- extremely simple
- easy to spell from hearing it
- backed by a verifiable linguistic rationale

## Anti-patterns — hard bans

### Startup name soup
Do not generate anything in this shape:

```
Aqnora  Nexora  Synaro  Vexora  Zyniq  Qorix  Axionyx
```

### Decorative letters
Never add X, Y, Z or Q to make a name look technological.

### Artificial suffixes
```
-ora  -ara  -iq  -ix  -ify  -ly  -nex  -syn  -ai  -tech  -soft  -labs
```

### Pseudo-Latin
`-um` and `-ium` endings with no real Latin word behind them read as
pharmaceutical, not premium. This is a common and expensive failure: a name
that scores well on structure and availability but sounds like a drug.

### Category leakage
The name must not need `Tech`, `Labs`, `Software`, `Digital` or `AI` to
read as a company. Test it bare:

```
NAME          not          NAME Technologies
```

## Phonetic target

Calibrate against the reference brands the user gave you. For most global
technology brands the shape that works is:

- 4–8 letters
- 1–3 syllables
- strong consonant attack
- open vowel nucleus
- ends in a vowel or a sonorant (`-n`, `-l`, `-r`, `-m`)
- no digraphs that collapse across languages (`lh`, `nh`, `ch` between
  PT/EN/ES) and no diacritics

## Saturation warning

Short, elegant words in the technology territory are extremely saturated.
Expect to eliminate most of a beautiful shortlist on collision grounds
alone. This is normal. Budget for at least two full rounds and do not treat
round-one survivors as finalists.
